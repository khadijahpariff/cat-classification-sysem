# Problem Statement

## Who is affected?
Cat owners, veterinary practitioners and animal shelter staff

## What exactly is going wrong?
Pet owners frequently struggle to accurately identify their cat's specific breed and recognize early signs of feline skin conditions (such as dermatophytosis/ringworm, flea allergy dermatitis, or alopecia). Because early dermatological symptoms visually overlap, non-expert owners rely on manual internet searches or delay seeking professional care, leading to disease progression and potential zoonotic transmission to humans or other household pets.

## Why does it matter?
Delayed diagnosis leads to severe medical complications, higher veterinary treatment costs, increased animal suffering, and unnecessary physical visits to overburdened veterinary clinics for minor visual assessments.

## Problem statement (3 to 5 sentences)
Cat owners and veterinary practitioners frequently face challenges in accurately identifying feline breeds and detecting early-stage skin health conditions due to a lack of accessible visual diagnostic tools. Non-expert owners often misinterpret overlapping dermatological symptoms or delay seeking professional care, allowing manageable skin infections to progress into severe complications. This delayed intervention increases overall veterinary treatment costs, heightens animal distress, and risks cross-contamination to other pets and humans. For instance, clinical veterinary studies show that over 40% of feline dermatological cases suffer from delayed treatment simply due to initial visual misassessment by pet owners.
